0. THESIS OF THE PRODUCT — UPDATED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The prior build read as a polemic: "Screen housing groups, not just individuals."
CommonGround v2 reads as operational property-management software. The thesis
has not changed — a household is still underwritten as a single financial unit —
but the voice shifts from advocacy to operator:

  • Copy everywhere uses PM / lending / real-estate vocabulary (rent roll,
    aggregate DTI, front-end ratio, QM wall, residual income, allocation
    addendum, deal memo, pro-rata, individualized assessment, trajectory).
  • Every screen is paired to a deliverable — a document a PM would actually
    produce, sign, or file. The Deal memo tab produces a real multi-page PDF.
    The Allocation tab produces a signable lease addendum.
  • Visual language moves from glass-morphism over a periwinkle gradient to
    editorial restraint on a warm cream (#FAF9F7) surface, with serif display
    type (Instrument Serif) on headlines and Outfit retained for UI.

The app now looks like the sort of thing a boutique PM firm could show a
prospect without apology.

  client/src/styles/dashboard.css `:root` block was updated to alias the

  --glass-radius) onto the new surface tokens. This cascades the flat

  zeros `backdrop-filter` on all named glass surfaces — nav bars, cards,
  tabs, modals — and replaces decorative hover-transforms with
  shadow-on-hover only.

  tokens.css provides .ui-btn (primary/secondary/ghost/sm/lg, plus
  ui-btn-split), .ui-pill (approved/conditional/review/ineligible/info/

  .ui-card-sunken, .ui-callout (+info/warning/success/danger variants
  with label + body), .ui-skeleton with shimmer animation, .ui-empty,
  .ui-serif / .ui-serif-tight, .ui-hairline.

  These now appear in Dashboard vacancy buttons, ReportTab, the Safety
  HUD chips, and anywhere a new element was introduced. The legacy
  .btn / .btn-primary / .status-badge rules continue to render correctly
  against the new tokens — a deliberate two-step migration.
